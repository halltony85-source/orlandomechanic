How Much Should a Mobile Mechanic Charge an Hour in Orlando?

What Orlando mechanics really earn, why the guides disagree on hourly rates, and the Florida estimate rules that protect you before any work starts.

Pricing Published 2026-10-15

There is no official hourly rate. Ask for the total price of the job in writing, because in Florida that estimate is what protects you.

Key takeaways

  • Orlando auto techs earned a median $23.00 an hour in May 2025 (BLS)
  • A billed rate also pays for the truck, tools, drive time and taxes
  • Over $150, Florida law says you get a written estimate first
  • The bill can only go $50 over the estimate without your okay

Everybody wants one number. Type this question into Google and you get Reddit threads, Facebook groups and a few guides that quote $50, $85 or $150 an hour. We read the top ones on October 12, 2026. None of them said where their numbers came from, and none of them said a word about Florida.

So here is what we can show you with real sources. What Orlando mechanics actually earn, why a mobile rate is built differently from a shop rate, and the Florida law that matters more than any hourly number.

Why Nobody Agrees on the Hourly Rate

The guides disagree because they are guessing in public. One 2026 guide from a mechanic software company puts the national mobile rate at $85 an hour and says rates run from $50 to $150. It credits industry data from thousands of mechanics but never names it. Another 2026 guide from a shop software maker says mobile mechanics charge $80 to $140 and calls its numbers estimates. Both may be fair guesses. Neither is something you can check.

The rest of page one is Reddit threads, Facebook groups, a forum and a TikTok, where mechanics trade their own rates and argue about them. Those are real opinions from real mechanics. They are not a market rate.

There is a better place to start. The government tracks what mechanics get paid.

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics runs a wage survey every May. Its May 2025 numbers count 6,480 automotive service technicians and mechanics working in the Orlando, Kissimmee and Sanford metro area. Here is what they earned, next to Florida and the whole country.

Two things jump out. Orlando mechanics earn a little less than the national median. And the mean is higher than the median, which means a smaller group of top techs pulls the average up. That matches what you see on the street. A master tech with a diagnostic scope and years on Toyotas and Fords is not paid like a lube tech.

Now the key point. That wage is not the price you pay. It is what lands in the mechanic's pocket before anything else gets paid.

How a Mobile Rate Gets Built

A labor rate has to cover the mechanic's pay and everything that makes the work possible. For a shop that means rent, lifts and a front desk. For a mobile mechanic it is a different list.

  • The truck. Fuel, tires, insurance and repairs on the van that carries the shop.
  • Tools and scan gear. A real diagnostic scanner, software updates and the hand tools that ride along every day.
  • Drive time. Nobody bills for sitting on I-4. An hour in traffic between jobs is an hour of pay with no work in it.
  • Parts runs. Picking up the right part, and taking back the wrong one, is time on the clock too.
  • Taxes and business costs. The things every business pays before the owner sees a dollar.

Here is a worked example, not anyone's real price. Say a mobile mechanic wants to earn the Orlando median of $23 an hour. Say a third of the workday goes to driving, parts runs and paperwork that nobody pays for. To clear $23 for every hour worked, he has to bill $34.50 for every hour he is on a car. That is before one gallon of gas, one tool, one insurance bill or one tax payment. Add those and you can see how the rates in the guides get where they are.

There is one place mobile repair can come out ahead. There is no building to pay for and no waiting room to heat or cool. Whether that saving reaches you depends on the job and the mechanic. So do not judge by the rate. Judge by the total.

The Hourly Rate Is the Wrong Thing to Compare

Most repair work is not timed with a stopwatch. A lot of mechanics, at shops and on the road, price a job from a set labor time for that repair on that car, or quote a flat price for the whole job. Florida law even makes the shop tell you which one you are getting. A written estimate has to say whether you are charged a flat rate, an hourly rate, or both.

That is why two quotes with the same hourly rate can land far apart, and two quotes with very different rates can come out the same. Here is what to put side by side instead.

The Florida Rules That Protect You

This is the part none of the top pages cover, and it is worth more than any hourly number. Florida has a law just for car repair. It is the Florida Motor Vehicle Repair Act, Chapter 559, Part IX of the Florida Statutes.

Mobile Mechanics Count as Repair Shops

Section 559.903 defines a repair shop as anyone paid to fix cars owned by other people. It names mobile motor vehicle repair shops and self-employed people right in the list. Section 559.904 says each repair shop has to register with the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services before doing business. The department has a Business License Lookup on its website, so you can check a name before you hand over a key.

You Get a Written Estimate Over $150

Under section 559.905, when a repair will cost you more than $150, the shop has to give you a written estimate before any diagnosis or repair begins. The estimate has to show the shop's name and phone, your car's details, a description of the problem, flat or hourly, the estimated cost, the charge for making the estimate, any guarantee, and whether you want your old parts saved. You are allowed to waive the estimate, but you do not have to.

The Bill Can Only Go a Little Over

Section 559.909 caps the surprise. Without your okay, the final bill can only go over the written estimate by $10 or 10 percent, whichever is more, and never by more than $50. If more work turns up, the shop has to call you, and you can say yes, change the job, or cancel it. The shop also cannot keep your car because you refused to pay for work you never agreed to.

Here is what that cap looks like on real dollar amounts. These are worked examples from the statute, not prices for any job.

Read that table again with the hourly rate question in mind. A written estimate with a total on it protects you no matter what the rate is. An hourly rate with no estimate protects you from nothing.

You Can See Your Old Parts

The same section says you can ask to inspect the parts that came off your car, as long as you ask when you approve the work. In many cases you can take them home. It is the easiest way to know the part was really replaced.

How to Get a Fair Price This Week

  1. Describe the problem clearly

    The year, make, model and mileage, what you hear or feel, and when it happens. A clear story gets a closer quote.

  2. Ask for the total, not the rate

    Ask what the whole job costs, parts and labor, and whether that is flat or hourly.

  3. Ask about diagnosis and trip charges

    Find out up front if finding the problem costs extra, and if it counts toward the repair.

  4. Get it in writing over $150

    It is Florida law. A text or email with the total is better than a number said in passing.

  5. Check the registration

    Look the business up on the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services Business License Lookup.

  6. Ask for your old parts

    Say so when you approve the work, not after.

Questions people ask

How much should a mobile mechanic charge an hour?

There is no official number. Guides that rank for this question quote anywhere from $50 to $150 an hour, and the ones we read on October 12, 2026 did not name where their numbers came from. The hourly rate also matters less than the total. Ask for the price of the whole job, in writing, before work starts.

How much is mechanic labor per hour in Florida?

What a mechanic earns and what a shop bills are two different numbers. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics says the median auto technician in Florida earned $23.20 an hour in May 2025. A billed labor rate is higher because it also pays for tools, insurance, the truck or the building, and the hours nobody can bill.

Is $150 an hour expensive for a mechanic?

Not by itself. A $150 rate on a job booked at one hour costs less than a $90 rate on a job that somehow takes three. Compare the full price for the same job, the same parts and the same guarantee. Then the hourly rate stops mattering.

Can a mechanic charge more than the estimate in Florida?

Only by a little, unless you say yes first. Florida law lets the final bill go over a written estimate by $10 or 10 percent, whichever is more, but never by more than $50. Past that, the shop has to call you and get your okay.

Do mobile mechanics have to register in Florida?

Yes. The Florida Motor Vehicle Repair Act counts mobile repair shops and self-employed mechanics as repair shops, and repair shops have to register with the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services. You can look a business up on the department's Business License Lookup.

Do I have to pay for a diagnosis?

Often, yes. Finding the problem is real work. In Florida, a written estimate has to list the charge for making the estimate, or how that charge is worked out. Ask about it on the phone so it is not a surprise.
